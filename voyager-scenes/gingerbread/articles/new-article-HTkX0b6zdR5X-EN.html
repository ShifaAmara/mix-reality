In the 16th century, Germany also made a breakthrough. They baked gingerbread cookies and shaped them to resemble houses or cabins in winter, complete with chimneys and white snow. From this point on, gingerbread cookies became closely associated with Christmas celebrations and the end of the year holidays, which always fall in winter. Today, gingerbread cookies have undergone modifications in their composition. Sometimes they are spiced with pepper, cinnamon, palm sugar, and other spices. Their shapes are also diverse, ranging from people, pine trees, stars, to Santa's reindeer.
